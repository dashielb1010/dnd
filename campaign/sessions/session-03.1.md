# Session 3.1 — The Flute and the Forest

## Synopsis
A parallel intro session for Kai and Casey-M's characters. A Dragonborn monk meditating at the edge of his monastery's forest was mysteriously transported deep into the Wealdath. He met Cornbread — a strange, cheerful elf who takes forest-displacement in stride — and the two wandered together toward the northern coast of the Dragon's Head, ending up about 8 miles west of Velen.

## What Happened

1. **Kai's character** lived in a monastery along the southern edge of the Duchy of Durmista. A contemplative life — daily training and meditation, pastoral community, caring for travelers on the southern road.
2. One evening after training, he ventured out alone and lit a bonfire at the forest's edge with his dragonbreath. He sat and played his flute, letting the music carry him into a deep meditative trance.
3. The trance deepened beyond anything he'd experienced. The forest seemed to grow around him. The firelight faded. He leaned into the sensation, curious rather than afraid.
4. When he resurfaced — what felt like hours later — everything had changed. His fire was gone. The trees were unfamiliar. The air smelled different: fresh, wild, with salt on it.
5. He flew above the canopy and saw an entirely unfamiliar landscape stretching in every direction, with ocean to the north. He was somewhere on the Dragon's Head peninsula, though he didn't know it yet.
6. With characteristic calm, he descended and began walking toward the sea.
7. In the forest he encountered **Cornbread** — a petite elvish woman with a tiny bat companion and a sentient log that followed her around like a dog. After introductions, Kai explained what had happened to him.
8. Cornbread's response: *"Oh yeah, that'll happen! That's happened to me a few times! I like it. I like to wander to new places."*
9. They decided, quite naturally, that they'd be alright wandering together for a while. And so they carried on toward the ocean.

## Key Decisions
- Kai leaned into the trance rather than resisting — whatever happened, he chose it (at least partly)
- Both characters chose companionship over solitude without hesitation — an easy, natural pairing
